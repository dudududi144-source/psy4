## Section 1 — Typical 6–8 Minute Arrangement (Section Sequence + Bar Counts)

### 1.1 Cross-source section model

Cross-referenced against MYL, EDMGHOST, IDM, PSYGUIDE, MEL-GOA, MEL-PROG, WIKI (via RESEARCH-A), and REDDIT-PSY. The convergence across sources is strong on the *sequence* of sections; bar counts vary by ±50% by subgenre and producer preference.

A typical 6–8 minute psytrance track at ~140 BPM (1 bar ≈ 1.71 s; 1 minute ≈ 35 bars; 7 minutes ≈ 245 bars) contains **9–12 sections** drawn from the following ordered set:

| # | Section | Typical bars (140 BPM) | Typical clock time | Active role set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intro** | 32–64 | ~55 s – 1 min 50 s | Kick + minimal percussion; bass enters mid-intro (or at end); atmospheric pad hint |
| 2 | **Groove Establishment** | 16–32 | ~30–55 s | Bass + hats/shakers/rides + mid-percussion; full rhythm bed |
| 3 | **First Statement / Main Groove** | 32–64 | ~55 s – 1 min 50 s | First lead or acid/FM motif enters; counterline may follow |
| 4 | **Development 1** | 32–64 | ~55 s – 1 min 50 s | Counterline, plucks/stabs, arpeggios; texture/FX layer thickens |
| 5 | **Breakdown 1** | 16–32 | ~30–55 s (often cited as "30 s – 1 min+") | Kick + bass DROP OUT; pads/drones + texture + filtered FX + (often) main melodic hook introduced |
| 6 | **Build-up / Riser** | 8–16 | ~15–30 s | Riser FX, snare roll, filter sweeps, pitch risers, kick often removed near end |
| 7 | **Drop 1 / Main Section** | 32–64 | ~55 s – 1 min 50 s | Full ensemble returns: kick + bass + lead + counterline + percussion + atmosphere + FX |
| 8 | **Development 2 / Variation** | 32–64 | ~55 s – 1 min 50 s | Transformed lead, new counterline, motif variation, possible key/registral shift |
| 9 | **Breakdown 2** (optional in shorter tracks) | 16–32 | ~30–55 s | Second reset; sometimes shorter than Breakdown 1 |
| 10 | **Build-up 2** | 8–16 | ~15–30 s | As Build-up 1, often more intense |
| 11 | **Drop 2 / Final Drop** | 32–64 | ~55 s – 1 min 50 s | Climax; full ensemble; motif typically varied from Drop 1 |
| 12 | **Outro** | 32–64 | ~55 s – 1 min 50 s | Strip layers gradually: ensemble → bass + percussion → kick only; mirrors intro in reverse |

### 1.2 Source-by-source bar-count evidence

- **MYL** explicitly gives the intro timeline: *"0–16 bars Basic kick drum and minimal percussion; 16–32 bars Hi-hats and core rhythmic layers; 32–48 bars Atmospheric pads and subtle melodic hints; 48–64 bars Full percussion and transition effects."* Intro = *"typically lasting 32 to 64 bars"*. Breakdown = *"often around 32 bars long"*. Outro = *"over 32 to 64 bars, mirroring the intro in reverse"*. Phase durations: *"Introduction 1–2 minutes… Main Section 2–3 minutes… Breakdown 1–2 minutes… Peak/Drop 1–2 minutes… Outro 1 minute"*. A concrete bar-map example is given: *"1–16 Kick drum with minimal percussion / 17–32 Add hi-hats / 33–48 Layer in pads / 57–64 Breakdown / 73–80 Drop / 97–105 Start outro"*.
- **EDMGHOST** gives generic dance-music lengths: intro *"16 or 32 bars"*, build-up *"8–16 bars"*, drop *"16–32 bars"*, breakdown *"16–32 bars"*, bridge *"8–16 bars"*, outro *"16 or 32 bars"*. (Note: EDMGHOST's drop bar counts are lower than typical psytrance drops — psytrance drops are commonly 32–64 bars because the genre sustains the main section longer than pop-EDM.) EDMGHOST confirms two canonical templates: *DJ-friendly club arrangement = intro → (build) → drop/main → breakdown → build → drop → outro*; *mainstage/big-room = intro → build-up → drop → breakdown → build-up → second drop → outro* — *"two waves of tension and release"*.
- **WIKI** (via RESEARCH-A): *"Layering is used to create effect in psychedelic trance, with new musical ideas being added at regular intervals, often every four to eight bars"*; breakdown *"30 seconds – 1 minute or more"*.
- **MEL-GOA** explicitly: *"Aim for 8–12 minute structures with multiple sections: long DJ-friendly intro, incremental layering, 1–3 breakdowns, and progressively higher‑energy peaks."*
- **MEL-PROG**: *"Think in long arcs: introduce and retire elements gradually; craft extended intros/outros for DJ mixing. Use one or two main breakdowns… focused, groovy drop rather than maximalist peaks."*
- **REDDIT-PSY**: *"8–32 bar sections"*; recommended hitech tutorial applies to most psy.
- **REDDIT-STRUCT**: *"a super simplified example skeleton of a full 64 beat phrase would be something like 8-8-8-8-cymbalcrash-8-8-8-8-drop new melody/beat pattern"* — i.e. an 8-bar phrase of 4×(2-bar) groups, a crash, then a second 8-bar phrase with a new pattern.

### 1.3 Synthesis: the 9–12 section skeleton

The minimum **musically complete** psytrance arrangement contains:

> INTRO → GROOVE → STATEMENT → DEVELOPMENT → BREAKDOWN → BUILD → DROP 1 → DEVELOPMENT 2 → (BREAKDOWN 2 → BUILD 2 →) DROP 2 → OUTRO

Sections marked `(…)` are optional in shorter tracks (~6 min) and present in longer ones (~8 min+). A 6-minute track often has **one** breakdown+drop+second-drop sequence (drop 1 and drop 2 separated by a short variation rather than a full second breakdown). An 8-minute track typically has **two** full breakdown+drop cycles.

**Verdict (Section 1):** The current Foundation's `SectionRole` enum (ESTABLISH / REPEAT_VARIATION / DEVELOPMENT / CONTRAST / RETURN / ESCALATION / PEAK / RELEASE) describes *section-purpose* but not *section-type* in the arrangement sense (INTRO / GROOVE / BREAKDOWN / BUILD / DROP / OUTRO). A complete WHAT-layer needs BOTH: section-type (where in the form) + section-purpose (what musically happens there). This is a *structural-model gap*, complementary to RESEARCH-A's role-set gap.

## Section 2 — Energy / Tension Arc Patterns

### 2.1 Multi-arc wave, not single arc

A psytrance track is **NOT a single tension arc**. The consensus across MYL, EDMGHOST, EDMPROD, HYPERBITS, and WIKI is a **wave-like multi-arc structure** with 2–3 peaks (drops), each preceded by a build and followed by a release/breakdown.

- **EDMGHOST**: *"delivering two waves of tension and release with mixable bookends at each end."*
- **HYPERBITS**: *"A good breakdown provides a momentary pause and an opportunity to reset the energy level of the track after a high-intensity drop section."*
- **MYL** phase-energy table explicitly: Introduction = Low; Main Section = Medium; Breakdown = Variable; Peak/Drop = High; Outro = Decreasing.
- **EDMPROD**: *"Dance music relies on three main elements: tension, energy, and groove."* Build-ups *"stretch the listener to a point where there's only one thing that can satisfy the emotional need cultivated by tension. A resolution, an answer to the question."*

### 2.2 Multi-level tension

Tension/release operates simultaneously at three temporal levels:

| Level | Time scale | Mechanism | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Macro (section-level)** | 32–64 bars (~1–2 min) | Arc from breakdown (low) → build (rising) → drop (peak) → development (sustained high) → next breakdown (release) | EDMGHOST, MYL |
| **Meso (phrase-level)** | 8–32 bars | Layer addition/removal at phrase boundaries; motif variation; filter sweeps across 8–32 bars | MYL, MEL-PROG, WIKI |
| **Micro (bar/beat-level)** | 1–8 bars | Crash cymbal at bar 4 of an 8-bar phrase; removing kick at end of 8 bars; snare-roll doubling; ghost notes; velocity accents | EDMPROD, REDDIT-STRUCT, ECLIP |

EDMPROD explicitly: *"Micro-tension (crash cymbals, removing kicks at the end of 8 bars, etc.) drives the song along."* This is the bar-level complement to the section-level arc.

### 2.3 Energy profile by subgenre (synthesis from MEL-* and PSYGUIDE)

- **Full-on**: clear multi-arc; euphoric climax peaks; strong contrast between breakdown (low) and drop (high). Energy curve: `low → medium → high → low → high → low`.
- **Progressive**: gradual evolution; less dramatic dynamic swings; "incremental modulation over time" (MEL-PROG). Energy curve: `low → medium → medium-high → medium → medium-high → low` (flatter, groove-sustained).
- **Dark/Darkpsy**: sustained high tension; less euphoric release; relentless (Vitos 2009: *"organic evolution and breakdown of distorted and interwoven sound layers"*).
- **Goa**: long building arc with multiple progressively-higher peaks (MEL-GOA: *"progressively higher‑energy peaks"*).
- **Forest**: continuous hypnotic groove; *"emphasize continuous flow and gradual transformations"* (MEL-FOREST); less dramatic breakdown-release swing.
- **HiTech**: restless, frenetic; non-arc; *"modular sequences of tension, rupture, and release"* (MEL-HITECH).

## Section 3 — Phrase Structure Conventions

### 3.1 Standard phrase = 8 bars (32 beats); multiples of 8 (16, 32)

Cross-referenced across EDMGHOST-BARS, VIBESDJ, MYL, WIKI:

- **EDMGHOST-BARS**: *"In dance music the standard phrase is 8 bars long. Since each bar is 4 beats, that means 8 bars equals 32 beats in a phrase. Four-bar and sixteen-bar groupings [are also used]."*
- **VIBESDJ**: *"Most dance tracks follow repeating phrase lengths in multiples of four bars, often 8, 16, or 32. This lets you predict safe mix-in and mix-out [points]."*
- **MYL**: *"Use 8-bar sections for smooth transitions and 16-bar sections to build momentum."*; *"Add new sounds or layers every 8 bars to maintain interest."*
- **WIKI** (via RESEARCH-A): *"new musical ideas being added at regular intervals, often every four to eight bars."*
- **REDDIT-STRUCT**: 8-bar phrase composed of 4 × 2-bar groups (`8-8-8-8-cymbalcrash-8-8-8-8-drop`).

### 3.2 Phrase grouping into larger structures

| Level | Bars | Beats | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro-phrase | 4 | 16 | Smallest structural unit; sometimes used for fills/turnarounds |
| **Phrase** | **8** | **32** | **Standard psytrance phrase**; layer-entry boundary; main modulation unit |
| Double-phrase | 16 | 64 | Momentum-building; motif variation; small section |
| Hyper-phrase / Section | 32 | 128 | Section length (intro, drop, breakdown) |
| Long-arc / Macro-section | 64 | 256 | Long development; Goa "8–12 min" structures |

### 3.3 What happens at phrase boundaries

Boundary events serve as **structural signposts** (cross-source synthesis):

- **Crash cymbal / impact** on the downbeat of phrase 1 (REDDIT-STRUCT: *"cymbalcrash"*; EDMPROD: *"crash cymbals… at the end of 8 bars"*).
- **Layer addition or removal** (MYL: *"Add new sounds or layers every 8 bars"*; WIKI: 4–8 bar new ideas).
- **Filter sweeps / automation** reset (MYL, EDMPROD).
- **Removal of kick** at end of an 8-bar phrase to signal section change (EDMPROD: *"removing kicks at the end of 8 bars"*).
- **Riser resolution** — riser peaks on phrase downbeat (EDMGHOST, EDMPROD).
- **Motif transformation** — second 4 bars of an 8-bar phrase introduces 1–2 note variation (ECLIP: *"Keep the first 4 bars familiar, but in the second 4 bars, change one or two notes or add an octave shift"*).

## Section 4 — Layer Entry Cadence

### 4.1 The conventional entry order

Synthesised from MYL (explicit intro timeline), WIKI (4–8 bar cadence), MEL-PROG (8–32 bar phrase modulation), and the role taxonomy in RESEARCH-A. The conventional layering order is:

| Phase | Enters | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 0–16 bars | **Kick** + minimal percussion (often just kick + occasional hat) | MYL |
| 16–32 bars | **Bass** (rolling 1/16) + **hi-hats** + core rhythmic layers | MYL, WIKI |
| 32–48 bars | **Atmospheric pads** + subtle melodic hints (texture/atmosphere) | MYL |
| 48–64 bars | **Full percussion** (mid-perc, claps, snares, rimshots, toms) + transition FX | MYL |
| 64+ bars | **Lead / acid / FM motif** (first statement) | Inferred from MYL + RESEARCH-A role order |
| 80+ bars | **Counterline / call-response / plucks / stabs / arpeggios** | Inferred from MYL + RESEARCH-A |
| Pre-drop | **Riser / sweep / impact** (transition FX) | EDMPROD, EDMGHOST |
| Drop (re-entry) | **Full ensemble simultaneously** | EDMGHOST, MYL |

### 4.2 Cadence rate

- **MYL**: *"Add new sounds or layers every 8 bars."* — i.e. **8-bar cadence**.
- **WIKI** (via RESEARCH-A): *"new musical ideas being added at regular intervals, often every four to eight bars."*
- **MEL-PROG**: *"automate filters, FX sends, and LFO depths over 8–32 bar phrases."* — i.e.**8-bar entry cadence, 16–32 bar modulation cadence**.

### 4.3 Conventional order (summary)

> **kick → bass → hats/shakers/rides → mid-percussion → pads/atmosphere → lead/acid → counterline/plucks/arps → FX (risers/sweeps/impacts)**

This is **not** universal law — it is the *common convention*. Subgenres vary:
- **Forest** layers foley/organic textures earlier and throughout (MEL-FOREST).
- **Goa** layers arpeggios and acid earlier and more continuously (MEL-GOA).
- **Progressive** layers fewer elements and adds them more gradually (MEL-PROG).
- **HiTech** layers in 16–32 bar modular episodes with frequent stop-cuts rather than linear build (MEL-HITECH).

## Section 5 — Breakdown Mechanics

### 5.1 What happens in a breakdown

Psytrance-specific source **IDM** (Dash Glitch) is explicit:

> *"Removing main elements of the track in the breakdown, such as the bassline, creates a much harder hitting effect when it drops back in again. This also allows you to add elements like risers or low-end thumps, which may clash with the bass or over-power the mix usually, however because we created space in the breakdown, they help to create that building momentum."*

> *"Using effects like filters can really help to boost the overall momentum in your track. It's a great tool for slowly fading out elements before smashing them back in at the beginning of the next bar. Find me a Psytrance track that doesn't have this effect somewhere in it, and I will be impressed!"*

> *"Another effect that is commonly used in Psytrance breaks is a retrigger"* — naming **LoopMash, Beat-Repeat, Gross Beat, dBlue Glitch, Dmitry Sches Tantra**.

### 5.2 Role activity during breakdown

| Role | State in breakdown | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Kick | **OUT** (or heavily reduced / high-passed ghost) | MYL, IDM, EDMGHOST |
| Bass (rolling 1/16) | **OUT** (the defining removal in psytrance) | IDM, MYL |
| Hats / shakers / rides | OUT or reduced | MYL |
| Mid-percussion | OUT | MYL |
| Lead | Often OUT, sometimes transformed/hinted | MYL |
| Pads / drones | **SUSTAIN** (often foreground) | MYL, EDMGHOST |
| Texture / atmosphere | **SUSTAIN** (often foreground) | MED-MOD |
| FX (risers, sweeps, low-end thumps) | **ENTER** (build-up of momentum) | IDM |
| Retrigger / glitch FX | ENTER (psytrance-specific) | IDM |
| Main melodic hook | Often **INTRODUCED** here (preview before drop) | MYL: *"The main melodic hook is introduced"* |

### 5.3 Breakdown length

- **MYL**: ~32 bars.
- **EDMGHOST**: 16–32 bars.
- **WIKI** (RESEARCH-A): 30 s – 1 min+ (≈ 16–32 bars at 140 BPM).
- **CLUBRDY**: breakdown is the popular DJ mix-in point — implies it is long enough for a transition (8–32 bars).

### 5.4 Re-entry build (build-up mechanics)

After the breakdown, the **re-entry build** reconstructs energy. EDMPROD gives the toolkit:

- **Filters**: cutoff automation on synths (open low-pass / raise high-pass on master for impact); resonance for harshness.
- **Reverb**: automate dry/wet on risers/leads/snare rolls; increase decay length for tension.
- **Pitch**: rising pitch on vocals, risers, snare rolls, reverb tails.
- **Snare rolls**: doubling pattern (1/4 → 1/8 → 1/16 → 1/32) over 8 bars; *"the most simple way to create a snare roll is by doubling up the speed and shortening the length."*
- **Kick rolls** (same principles as snare rolls).
- **Alternative rhythms** for variation.
- **Bar of silence** for dramatic effect before drop (EDMGHOST).

**MYL** on the build: *"Start with high-passed kicks and atmospheric sounds. Gradually bring in rhythmic elements like snare rolls and risers to intensify the energy. Use automation: Adjust reverb levels to add depth. Gradually open filter cutoffs for tension. Increase delay feedback to expand the soundstage."*

## Section 6 — Drop Mechanics

### 6.1 What makes a drop

- **EDMGHOST** (citing Wikipedia on the drop): *"the point where a sudden change of rhythm or bassline occurs — preceded by a build-up and a break — and it is the loudest portion of an EDM track."*
- **EDMGHOST**: *"the payoff — the loudest, most energetic part of the track, where the full arrangement, bassline, and main hook hit at once."*
- **MYL**: *"The kick and full percussion return alongside the main melody, delivering the track's high point."*
- **IDM**: *"create a break-down before it, thus releasing tension before allowing it to build up again."* — i.e. drop impact is *contingent on the preceding breakdown*, not on the drop alone.

### 6.2 Roles re-entering simultaneously

On the drop downbeat, the following re-enter **simultaneously** (cross-source synthesis):

| Role | Re-entry behaviour |
|---|---|
| Kick | Returns at full intensity |
| Bass (rolling 1/16) | Returns at full intensity (the defining re-entry — IDM) |
| Full percussion (hats, shakers, mid-perc) | Returns |
| Lead / main motif | Stated (often the melodic hook previewed in breakdown — MYL) |
| Counterline / call-response | Often enters |
| Atmosphere / pad / drone | Often sustained (not removed) |
| Impact / crash | Hits on drop downbeat |
| FX riser tail | Decays from build-up |

### 6.3 Drop texture / arrangement

- **Full ensemble** at high density.
- **Main motif stated clearly** (often the strongest statement in the track).
- **Riser impact / crash** on the downbeat punctuates the entry.
- **Filter opens fully** on lead (after build's filter sweep opened it).
- **Bass + kick re-coupled** (timing lock + sidechain envelope re-engaged — see RESEARCH-A Section 4).

### 6.4 Second-drop variation

- **EDMGHOST**: *"The second drop typically varies the first slightly — a changed hook, bassline, or drum pattern — to keep interest without breaking the formula."*
- This is the primary locus of **motif transformation** at section level.

### 6.5 Subgenre drop character

- **Full-on**: maximalist drop; melodic lead front-and-centre; clear peak.
- **Progressive**: *"focused, groovy drop rather than maximalist peaks"* (MEL-PROG).
- **Goa**: gradual transformation; *"emphasize gradual transformation over abrupt drops"* (MEL-GOA).
- **Forest**: no real breakdown/drop swing; *"almost with no breakdowns and beat interruptions"* (PSYGUIDE).
- **Darkpsy**: relentless; no euphoric drop release.
- **HiTech**: *"modular sequences of tension, rupture, and release"* — drop is one of many micro-events, not a singular peak.

## Section 7 — Subgenre Differences (Arrangement, Role Set, Energy, Motif)

### 7.1 Comparison table (6 subgenres)

| Subgenre | BPM | Arrangement | Role set | Energy profile | Motif behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Full-on** (PSYGUIDE, MEL-HITECH comparison) | 140–148 | Classic intro→build→drop→breakdown→build→drop→outro; ~6–8 min; 2 main drops; bassline plays on various notes across octaves | Full: melodic bassline, lead, counterline, mid-perc, pads, FX | Clear multi-arc; euphoric climax peaks; strong breakdown→drop contrast | Catchy hooks, elaborate synth work, melodic leads; motif varies across octaves; second drop varies first |
| **Progressive** (MEL-PROG) | 130–138 | Long arcs; extended intros/outros; one or two main breakdowns; focused groovy drop, not maximalist | Fewer layers; tight low-end; rolling bass, hats/shakers/rides, evolving FM leads, pads/drones | Gradual evolution; subtle build; less dramatic swing; continuous groove | Short motifs, call-and-response FX, intervallic riffs slowly morphing; automate over 8–32 bar phrases |
| **Dark / Darkpsy** (PSYGUIDE, DANCECULT) | 140–155 (Deep Darkpsy 148–160) | Dense, aggressive; less song-form; more linear/hypnotic; fewer dramatic breakdowns; continuous texture | Heavy distorted bass, dense FM/acid textures, abstract sound design; minor modes, chromatic | Sustained high tension; less euphoric release; relentless | Abstract/dissonant motifs; experimental; *"organic evolution and breakdown of distorted and interwoven sound layers"* (Vitos 2009) |
| **Goa** (MEL-GOA, PSYGUIDE) | 135–150 | 8–12 min; long DJ-friendly intro; incremental layering; 1–3 breakdowns; progressively higher-energy peaks; gradual transformation over abrupt drops | TB-303 acid, layered arpeggios, shimmering pads, rapid ornamented leads, ethnic/field samples | Long building arc; multiple progressively-higher peaks | Raga-like motifs, rapid ornamented lead lines, call-and-response arpeggios; motifs evolve via filter automation; recurring motifs reshaped across arrangement |
| **Forest** (MEL-FOREST, PSYGUIDE) | 145–152 (PSYGUIDE: 150–160) | *"Relatively straightforward song structures"* — intro → rolling sections → modulated peaks → outro; *"almost with no breakdowns and beat interruptions"* (PSYGUIDE); brief breathers; continuous flow | Organic foley, swarming synth motifs, woody bark timbres, FM/PM, granular; *"ecosystems of small motifs"* | Continuous hypnotic groove; less dynamic swing; immersive not aggressive | Many interlocking micro-events fluttering/scuttling around stereo field; continuous slow transformation |
| **HiTech** (MEL-HITECH, ACM-HITECH) | 170–200+ | *"Fluid and often unpredictable"*; modular episodes 16–32 bars with frequent micro-breaks, fills, stop-cuts, pitch ramps; non-linear | Metallic FM tones, granular splinters, bitcrush, formant, robotic vox; tight rolling bass; percussive stabs + long FX tails | Restless, frenetic; *"tension, rupture, and release"* rather than big build-drop; high-density throughout | Non-narrative; abrupt switch-ups; metric feints; call-and-response between high glitches and mid growls |

### 7.2 Key axes of subgenre variation (synthesis)

1. **Song-form strength**: Full-on = strong; Goa = strong but extended; Progressive = strong but flatter; Darkpsy = weak (linear); Forest = weak (continuous); HiTech = weak (modular).
2. **Breakdown presence**: Full-on/Goa/Progressive = clear breakdowns; Darkpsy = few; Forest = brief breathers only; HiTech = micro-breaks.
3. **Drop drama**: Full-on = maximalist; Progressive = groovy; Goa = gradual transformation; Darkpsy/Forest = no euphoric drop; HiTech = rupture events.
4. **Motif type**: Full-on = melodic hooks; Progressive = short morphing motifs; Goa = ornamented raga-like; Darkpsy = abstract dissonant; Forest = swarming micro-events; HiTech = metallic glitches.
5. **Layer count**: Progressive = fewest; Full-on = many; Goa = many + arpeggios; Darkpsy = dense; Forest = dense + organic; HiTech = densest + micro-edited.

## Section 8 — Motif Development Over Long Durations

### 8.1 E-Clip's framework (most explicit source)

E-Clip (https://www.eclipmusic.com/post/mastering-psytrance-melodies-rhythmic-patterns-accents-variations) gives a 3-axis framework:

**(a) Accent types** (shape the rhythm):
- **Strong-beat accents** — notes on downbeats (1, 2, 3, 4); steady and grounded.
- **Offbeat accents** — between beats; bouncy, groovy.
- **Syncopated accents** — before/after expected beats; dynamic, unpredictable.
- **Dynamic accents** — higher velocity or modulation; expressive.
- *"FM riffs are a great example where dynamic accents can make a one-note sequence feel alive by changing note lengths, modulating filters, or adjusting velocity."*

**(b) Odd-time cycles** (hypnotic motion against 4/4):
- 3-note cycle (melody repeats every 3 sixteenth notes).
- 5-note cycle.
- 7-note cycle.
- *"The melody never feels static because it shifts against the main beat. It creates a rolling, hypnotic groove that keeps evolving."*

**(c) Repetition-with-variation**:
- *"Keep the first 4 bars familiar, but in the second 4 bars, change one or two notes or add an octave shift to keep the listener engaged."*

### 8.2 Variation axes (what changes over time)

Synthesis across ECLIP, MEL-PROG, MEL-GOA, MED-MOD, YGP (RESEARCH-A):

| Axis | How it varies | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Pitch** | Octave shift; interval change; modal shift | ECLIP |
| **Rhythm** | Syncopated note added/removed; odd-time cycle introduced | ECLIP |
| **Register** | Move lead up/down an octave (typical second-drop escalation) | ECLIP, EDMGHOST (second-drop variation) |
| **Velocity** | Accent pattern shifted | ECLIP |
| **Timbre** | Filter cutoff, FM index, wavefolding, formant | MEL-PROG, MEL-HITECH |
| **Effect modulation** | Reverb, delay feedback, panning | ECLIP, MEL-PROG |
| **Harmonic context** | Pad/drone shifts harmonically | MED-MOD |
| **Contour / interval leap** | Motif preserves its contour but changes notes | MED-MOD |

### 8.3 Variation cadence (how often)

- **Bar-level**: 1–2 note change every 4 bars (ECLIP: *"first 4 bars familiar, second 4 bars change one or two notes"*).
- **Phrase-level**: filter/FX automation across 8–32 bar phrases (MEL-PROG).
- **Section-level**: motif transformed at second drop (EDMGHOST); motif "dissolves into pad" at breakdown (MED-MOD).
- **Macro-level**: motif reshaped across the entire arrangement via filter/envelope automation (MEL-GOA: *"Automate filters, envelopes, and effects to reshape recurring motifs across the arrangement"*).

### 8.4 Callback / recapitulation

- **MED-MOD** (modular approach): motifs are *"a palette, not a sequence"*; themes influence one another; a motif can dissolve into a pad; a drone can shift harmonically and carry the track into the breakdown; a bass line can contract into stop-time hits and then drop back in. *"Transitions evolve out of the material that is already living in the track."*
- **EDMGHOST**: second drop varies first (callback with variation).
- **YGP** (RESEARCH-A): *"Psy trance works because it balances repetition and variation. Too little variation, and the track feels flat. Too much, and the trance effect disappears."*

### 8.5 Long-form development summary

A motif in a 6–8 minute psytrance track typically:
1. **Enters** mid-intro or in first statement (32–80 bars in).
2. **Repeats with 1–2 note variation** every 4 bars during development.
3. **Transforms** (filter opens, register shifts, octave up) approaching the drop.
4. **Is stated strongly** at drop 1.
5. **Dissolves** or **transforms** in breakdown (may dissolve into pad/drone; or be previewed in filtered form).
6. **Returns varied** at drop 2 (different octave, different timbre, or counterline added).
7. **Recurs as callback** in outro (often stripped, filtered, or in lower register).

## Section 9 — Transition Elements

### 9.1 Inventory of transition FX (psytrance-specific)

Synthesis across IDM, EDMPROD, MYL, EDMGHOST, REDDIT-STRUCT, RESEARCH-A:

| FX type | Function | Placement | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Risers** | Upward pitch/frequency sweep; tension build pre-drop | Last 8–16 bars before drop | EDMGHOST, EDMPROD, IDM, SoundBridge (search snippet) |
| **Impacts / hits** | Single удар on section-start downbeat | Downbeat of drop / section 1 | EDMGHOST, IDM |
| **Sweeps (up-lifters / down-lifters)** | Frequency motion across section boundaries | At section transitions | EDMGHOST, RESEARCH-A |
| **Reverses** | Reversed tails as transition devices | Pre-drop, pre-section | RESEARCH-A |
| **Down-lifters** | Downward sweep; release after drop | Post-drop, into breakdown | EDMGHOST |
| **White-noise sweeps** | Textural build/release | Throughout build-up | EDMGHOST, EDMPROD |
| **Snare rolls** | Compounding rhythmic acceleration | Last 8 bars of build | EDMPROD, MYL |
| **Kick rolls** | Same as snare rolls but on kick | End of build | EDMPROD |
| **Filter sweeps** | Slowly fading elements out before smash-back-in | Pre-drop, pre-section, breakdown | IDM, MYL |
| **Retrigger / glitch FX** (LoopMash, Beat-Repeat, Gross Beat, dBlue Glitch, Tantra) | Psytrance-specific: stuttered/retriggered material in breakdown | In breakdown | IDM |
| **Crash cymbal** | Phrase-boundary punctuation | Bar 1 of phrase | REDDIT-STRUCT, EDMPROD |
| **Bar of silence** | Dramatic pause before drop | Last bar of build | EDMGHOST |
| **Delay throws** | Micro-variation; ear-candy | Throughout | RESEARCH-A |
| **Glitches** | Unexpected audio manipulation at transitions | At transitions | RESEARCH-A |

### 9.2 Are transitions musical roles or just FX?

**Both.** They serve two distinct musical functions:

1. **Structural signposting** — riser/impact/crash mark phrase and section boundaries. This is a *musical role* (a *transition-glue* functional group, per RESEARCH-A Section 1.4).
2. **Sound-design decoration** — ear-candy glitches, delay throws, granular sprinkles. This is more *FX decoration* than structural role.

The distinction matters for the WHAT-layer: **structural transition FX** must be modelled as first-class roles with scheduled entry times (e.g. "riser enters at bar 73, peaks at bar 80"); **decorative FX** can be left to PSY4's synthesis discretion.

### 9.3 When transitions occur

- **At phrase boundaries** (every 8 bars): crash, small sweep, filter reset.
- **At section boundaries** (every 16–32 bars): larger riser, impact, down-lifter.
- **Pre-drop** (last 8–16 bars of build): main riser, snare roll, filter sweep, pitch riser, kick roll, optional bar of silence.
- **Drop downbeat**: impact / crash.
- **In breakdown**: retrigger / glitch FX (psytrance-specific).

## Section 10 — Negative Space Conventions

### 10.1 Where psytrance uses negative space

| Location | What is removed | What remains | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Breakdown** (16–32 bars) | Kick, bass, full percussion, often lead | Pads, drones, atmosphere, texture, FX, (often) main hook preview | MYL, IDM, EDMGHOST, WIKI |
| **Outro** (32–64 bars) | Lead, counterline, mid-perc, pads, FX (gradual strip) | Bass + percussion, then kick only | MYL, EDMGHOST |
| **Intro** (first 16–32 bars) | Bass, lead, mid-perc, pads | Kick + minimal percussion only | MYL |
| **Bar of silence** pre-drop | Everything | Silence / reverb tail | EDMGHOST |
| **Kick removal at end of 8-bar phrase** | Kick only (1–2 beats) | Everything else | EDMPROD |
| **Sparse moments in forest/darkpsy** | Conventional breakdown not used; instead density thins briefly | Continuous bass + atmosphere | MEL-FOREST |

### 10.2 Formulaic vs free

- **Formulaic** at section level: breakdown and outro are conventional negative-space sections; almost every full-on, progressive, and goa track has them.
- **Free at sub-phrase level**: where exactly within a development section to thin layers is the producer's choice.
- **Subgenre-dependent**:
  - **Full-on, Goa, Progressive**: clear breakdowns (formulaic negative space).
  - **Forest**: *"Reserve breakdowns for brief breathers; emphasize continuous flow and gradual transformations"* (MEL-FOREST) — minimal negative space.
  - **Darkpsy**: continuous; negative space is textural (sparse moments within dense texture) rather than structural.
  - **HiTech**: micro-breaks and stop-cuts (negative space at the bar/beat level, not section level).
- **MED-MOD** explicitly: Psy elements (drones, atmospheres, textures) *"should drift and evolve on their own. Some may last thirty seconds. Some may stretch for a minute or more. The point is to let them breathe instead of locking them to a loop."* — i.e. negative space is also about *temporal freedom* of atmospheric layers, not just absence of layers.

### 10.3 Negative space = role-activity mask (links to RESEARCH-A)

RESEARCH-A Section 7 established: **negative space = per-section role-activity mask**. This report confirms and refines that:
- The role-activity mask is **section-typed** (intro / breakdown / outro / drop / development), not generic.
- The mask is **subgenre-parameterised** (forest has fewer breakdown-masked sections than full-on).
- The mask is **phrase-aware** within sections (kick removed at end of 8-bar phrase = a 1-beat role-activity change, not a section change).

## Section 11 — FACT vs INDUSTRY PRACTICE vs COMMON CONVENTION vs INFERENCE

### 11.1 Definitions used in this report

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FACT** | Measurable / directly observable from a recording or explicit in an authoritative reference (Wikipedia, academic source). |
| **INDUSTRY PRACTICE** | What working producers actually do, as documented in producer tutorials and masterclasses. May not be universal. |
| **COMMON CONVENTION** | What is typical across the genre, observable in many tracks but not formalised. |
| **INFERENCE** | Synthesis or extrapolation by this researcher from the above; not directly stated by any single source. |

### 11.3 Caveats and limits of this research

1. **Sources are producer tutorials, not statistical analyses.** No source measures the bar counts of, say, 100 released psytrance tracks. Bar counts here are what producers *teach* and *aim for*, which may differ from what released tracks *actually do*.
2. **Trance vs psytrance conflation.** MYL is titled "Trance Song Structure" and applies broadly to trance family including psytrance. Some bar counts (e.g. drop = 16–32 bars in EDMGHOST) reflect generic EDM/mainstage rather than psytrance specifically. Psytrance drops are typically longer (32–64 bars) — this is an INFERENCE from psytrance's longer-form aesthetic, not a directly cited figure.
3. **Subgenre boundaries are fuzzy.** PSYGUIDE itself notes Night Full-on vs Twilight may be split in future. Forest is described as a sub-style of darkpsy by some Reddit users. Subgenre assignment is interpretive.
4. **One producer's workflow ≠ universal law.** MED-MOD's modular approach (Themes/Flow/Psy as parallel sandboxes) is one producer's method; linear arrangement is also common. IDM's "work backwards from drop" is one producer's tip. ECLIP's 4-bar variation rule is one teacher's heuristic.
5. **Academic sources are scarce.** Only Vitos 2009 (Dancecult, darkpsy) was found. The rest of psytrance arrangement scholarship is producer-tutorial literature, not peer-reviewed musicology.
6. **Bar-count arithmetic.** All bar→time conversions assume 140 BPM exactly. Subgenres range 130–200+ BPM, so the same bar count yields very different clock times. The bar counts in Section 1 are correct; the clock-time conversions are approximate and subgenre-dependent.
7. **RESEARCH-A already covered** role taxonomy, kick+bass coupling, motif accent/cycle framework, identity profile, and a basic section model. This report deepens arrangement/structure/transition/negative-space/motif-development and adds subgenre comparison; it does NOT re-cover role taxonomy. Where RESEARCH-A's claims are used, they are cited as "(RESEARCH-A)".

### 11.4 Bottom line for the orchestrator

The current Foundation WHAT-layer (per RESEARCH-A and the PSY4-MUSICAL-WHAT-LAYER-DESIGN-AUDIT) describes:
- ✅ Bar-level section roles (`SectionRole` enum: ESTABLISH/DEVELOPMENT/PEAK/RELEASE/etc.)
- ✅ Phrase-level slots (`PhraseRole` enum: INTRO/STATEMENT/DEVELOPMENT/RESPONSE/BUILD/RELEASE/TRANSITION/RESOLUTION)
- ✅ Motif notes with transformHistory (backward-looking)
- ✅ Energy/tension/density (scalar)

It does NOT yet describe:
- ❌ **Section-TYPE** (INTRO / GROOVE / BREAKDOWN / BUILD / DROP / OUTRO) — distinct from section-purpose
- ❌ **Multi-arc energy profile** (wave-like with 2–3 peaks, not single arc)
- ❌ **Multi-level tension** (macro/meso/micro with explicit primitives)
- ❌ **Phrase-level boundary events** (crash/impact/riser-placement schedule)
- ❌ **Layer entry cadence** (8-bar cadence with conventional order)
- ❌ **Breakdown role-activity mask** (kick+bass OUT, pads+texture SUSTAIN, FX ENTER, hook preview)
- ❌ **Drop role-activity mask** (full ensemble simultaneous re-entry)
- ❌ **Transition role schedule** (riser enters at bar X, peaks at bar Y; impact on drop downbeat)
- ❌ **Forward-looking motif development plan** (variation at bar X, register shift at bar Y, callback at bar Z)
- ❌ **Subgenre-parameterised arrangement profile** (full-on multi-arc vs forest continuous vs hitech modular)
- ❌ **Negative-space section typing** (breakdown mask vs outro mask vs intro mask)

These 10 items are the **arrangement-level complement** to RESEARCH-A's 8 WHAT-layer items. Together they form the minimum complete WHAT-layer for long-form psytrance generation.
